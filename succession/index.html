Our view

When a critical role becomes vacant, the disruption can start on day one. A succession plan makes sure someone is ready, or that you know what to do if no one is.

Why now

The cost of an unplanned vacancy.

01

Decisions wait

Approvals and escalations can stall while the business confirms who has the authority to act.

02

Work and commitments slip

Customer issues and projects can lose time when key relationships or knowledge sit with one person.

03

Interim cover has a cost

Leaders absorb work alongside their own responsibilities. Temporary support or overtime may add to the cost.

04

Recovery continues after hiring

The incoming person needs time to learn the work, rebuild relationships and take on the full scope of the role.

A useful first exercise: for each critical role, estimate the cover costs, delayed work and the time needed to restore full capacity.

What the plan helps you decide

Four decisions, one shared picture.

01

Business continuity

Which roles need an interim plan now? Who can hold authority and keep work moving?

02

Successor readiness

Which internal candidates have evidence against the future role? Who needs more time, exposure or assessment?

03

Development investment

Which assignments will close specific gaps? Who will observe the work and review the evidence?

04

External options

Where should the organisation explore external talent or a different approach to cover?

POV facilitates the evidence review. Appointment decisions stay with your organisation’s authorised leaders.

A shared language

What “ready” means.

We agree these time horizons with you before any assessment begins, so everyone reads the plan the same way.

Ready now

Current evidence supports taking on the full role with normal transition support.

Ready in 1–2 years

Specific gaps appear addressable through agreed assignments and observable milestones.

Longer-term prospect

Needs broader experience or sustained development before a timeframe can be confirmed.

Insufficient evidence

More assessment or work evidence is needed before estimating readiness.

No identified successor

No named internal candidate for the role yet. This is recorded against the role.

Alongside readiness, we also record each person’s career interest, availability and how confident we are in the evidence.

When no one is ready

A plan for the gaps, too.

We look at the likely vacancy date, the time needed to develop a successor and who can hold interim authority.

A viable internal candidate has time to develop

Use a stretch assignment with an observer and a checkpoint. Confirm interim cover.

Decide: Can the business protect the time and exposure the candidate needs?

A vacancy may come before anyone is ready

Activate temporary cover. Confirm delegated authority and begin external mapping or search as needed.

Decide: Who holds authority on day one, and when does a search begin?

The work depends heavily on one person

Cross-train, document decisions and share out responsibilities where feasible.

Decide: Which duties can move, and who checks that cover works?

No viable internal candidate is identified

Test talent in adjacent teams and look at the external market. If the remit is unusually broad, consider changing the role design.

Decide: Do we build, hire or change how the work is organised?
