The questions we help answer

A track record is only part of the picture.

Past success shows what someone has done. Assessment explores how that experience carries into what comes next.

Readiness

Can this person lead at the scale and complexity the role requires?

Transferability

What did they personally contribute, and how will their strengths translate as the role, resources or business context change?

Risks under pressure

Where might effectiveness weaken when priorities conflict, expectations increase or stakeholders resist?

Conditions for success

What needs further validation, and what support or development would help the person succeed?

How ready is this person for an agreed future role, and what would prepare them?

An indicative readiness view, the experiences and capabilities still to develop, and clear points for reviewing progress.

How we reach a judgement

More than one source. Every conclusion explained.

We agree the success profile with you first, using your competency framework where you have one.

Evidence sources
  • Structured interview

    Decisions, personal contribution, outcomes and learning through real career examples.

  • Role-relevant exercise

    A case or role play to observe priorities, stakeholder handling and judgement.

  • Behavioural assessment

    Where agreed, a suitable tool to identify tendencies and questions to explore.

  • Existing evidence

    CV and authorised inputs, with the limits of each source kept visible.

From evidence to judgement
  1. 1
    Separate the sources

    What the person reports, what the assessor observes and what a profile suggests.

  2. 2
    Test the pattern

    Look for supporting evidence, contradictions and other explanations.

  3. 3
    Explain the implication

    Show how a strength or concern could affect this role.

  4. 4
    State the confidence

    Say what the evidence supports and what still needs validating.

The session

A respectful experience for each person.

Each person knows the purpose, format and who will see the findings before they begin.

  1. Before

    Briefing

    The purpose, format, language, use of information and any preparation.

  2. Part one

    Career discussion

    Real examples and the person’s own decisions.

  3. Part two

    Case or role play

    Preparation, discussion and reflection on a role-relevant situation.

  4. Close

    Next steps

    Clarification, questions and how feedback will work.

Allow about 3 hours, with short breaks as needed. Final design and length depend on the role and scope.

The right balance

Every role weighs things differently.

Sometimes experience matters most. Sometimes attitude and willingness to learn count for more. We agree the balance before we meet anyone, then apply the same standard to everyone.

Experience and attitude matter equally, and the evidence needs to support both.

Examples only. The balance is agreed for each role.

Responsible use

Fair to the person. Useful to the decision.

Assessment supports professional judgement. It does not guarantee performance or replace your other relevant checks.

  • Everyone is measured against the same criteria and the same standard.
  • Judgement is explained, so you can see and question the reasoning.
  • People know the purpose, the activities and who receives the findings.
  • Exercises stay relevant to the role, with reasonable adjustments.
  • Access to reports is agreed in advance, on a need-to-know basis.
  • Findings are not later used for a different purpose without discussion.
  • Limited evidence is stated as a gap, not treated as a weakness.
  • Your organisation makes the final decision.
