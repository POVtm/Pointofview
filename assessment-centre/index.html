How a day works

Evidence from more than one moment.

Seeing someone in different situations gives a fuller picture than one interview can. A typical day brings these moments together.

Think and work together

People think on their own first, then work as a group on a shared business case.

Present and explain

Each group presents to assessors, then individuals answer questions on their own reasoning.

Handle a real conversation

A one-to-one role play shows how each person responds in the moment, followed by structured reflection.

Bring the evidence together

Assessors rate independently first, then discuss the evidence and agree one view of each person.

Sample only: one format can take 12 participants through a day in three groups. Numbers, timings and exercises are agreed for your roles and available assessors.

Fair by design

The same chance for everyone.

A centre is only useful if you can trust the evidence. These safeguards are built in from the start.

  • Everyone receives the same brief, timing and scoring guide.
  • Assessors are trained in what to look for and how to record it.
  • Role-players follow agreed responses, so each person gets a fair test.
  • Ratings are recorded independently before the panel discusses them.
  • Each person is seen across several sources of evidence.
  • Appropriate adjustments are agreed in advance.
  • Contradictions and remaining gaps are made visible.
  • Your organisation makes the final appointment or promotion decision. Development findings support the agreed growth objectives.

From evidence to decision

From realistic exercises to useful conclusions.

  1. Before the day

    Purpose and criteria

    Confirm the role or development goals, relevant competencies and evidence sources, including any interview notes your team shares.

  2. The day

    Exercises

    The group case and role play add evidence of how each person works.

  3. Same day

    Calibration

    Assessors record judgements independently, compare evidence and identify what remains uncertain.

  4. Afterwards

    Profile

    A clear summary of the conclusion, the evidence and the priorities.

  5. Using the findings

    Next step

    For selection, you make the decision. For development, the findings inform feedback, practical actions and support.

A follow-up conversation is only needed where a specific evidence gap remains.
